# Grammar of $\sigma$-Normal Forms on $\lambda_{\mathrm{Env}\varepsilon}$

This document states and proves thesis Theorem 6 (§2.3.3, equation (2.32)), presented here as a **Lemma** as requested: the $\sigma$-normal forms of $\lambda_{\mathrm{Env}\varepsilon}$ (`docs/enve-sigma-normal-form.md`) are exactly the terms generated by a specific restricted grammar, built from the same seven constructors as `docs/enve-syntax.md` but disallowing exactly the shapes that a $\sigma$-rule (`docs/enve-reduction-sigma.md`) could fire on.

> **Note for AI-assisted formalization.**
> This grammar is *not* a separate datatype: it singles out, among all
> terms of $\mathbf{Term}(\lambda_{\mathrm{Env}\varepsilon})$, exactly those
> that are $\sigma$-irreducible (`docs/enve-sigma-normal-form.md`'s
> equivalent characterization of "being a $\sigma$-normal form"). A
> convenient way to formalize it is as an inductively-defined predicate
> `is_sigma_normal :: term \<Rightarrow> bool` mirroring the eight cases below,
> rather than as a second `datatype`.

---

## Lemma (Grammar of $\sigma$-Normal Forms)

A $\lambda_{\mathrm{Env}\varepsilon}$ term $M$ is a $\sigma$-normal form if and only if $M$ is generated by the grammar
$$
U ::= \mathsf{id} \mid x \mid \lambda x.\,U_1 \mid U_1\,U_2 \mid (U_1/x)\cdot U_2 \mid (\lambda x.\,U_1) \circ U_3 \mid x \circ W \mid \varepsilon(U_1)
\tag{2.32}
$$
where $x$ ranges over $\mathbf{Var}$; $U_1, U_2, U_3, W$ range over $\sigma$-normal forms of $\lambda_{\mathrm{Env}\varepsilon}$; $U_3 \neq \mathsf{id}$; and $W$ is neither $\mathsf{id}$ nor of the form $(P/y)\cdot Q$ (an environment extension) for any $P, y, Q$.

#### Proof.

Both directions are by structural induction.

### ($\Leftarrow$) Every term generated by (2.32) is $\sigma$-irreducible.

By induction on the derivation of $M$ from grammar (2.32). Recall that $M$ is $\sigma$-irreducible exactly when no $\sigma$-rule -- neither one of the eight base rules (`Assoc`, `IdL`, `IdR`, `DExtn`, `VarRef`, `VarSkip`, `DApp`, `Eps-eps`) nor one of the eight congruence rules (`Lam`, `Eop`, `AppL`, `AppR`, `ExtnL`, `ExtnR`, `CompL`, `CompR`) -- applies anywhere in $M$, i.e. $M$ has no $\sigma$-redex in any subterm.

- **$M = \mathsf{id}$ or $M = x$.** Neither term has any subterm to reduce, and neither matches the left-hand side of any base rule (every base rule's left-hand side is headed by `Comp`). Irreducible.

- **$M = \lambda x.\,U_1$, $M_1M_2$, $(U_1/x)\cdot U_2$, or $\varepsilon(U_1)$** (with $U_1, U_2$ $\sigma$-normal forms by hypothesis). None of the eight base rules has a left-hand side headed by `Lam`, `App`, `Ext`, or `Eps` -- every base rule's left-hand side is headed by `Comp` -- so no base rule fires at the root. By the induction hypothesis, $U_1$ (and $U_2$, where present) is itself free of $\sigma$-redexes, so no congruence rule fires inside them either. Irreducible.

- **$M = (\lambda x.\,U_1) \circ U_3$** (with $U_1, U_3$ $\sigma$-normal forms, $U_3 \neq \mathsf{id}$). Check each base rule against the root shape $\mathrm{Comp}(\lambda x.\,U_1,\, U_3)$: `Assoc` needs the left argument to be `Comp`-headed (it is `Lam`-headed) -- does not fire; `IdL` needs the left argument to be $\mathsf{id}$ -- does not fire; `IdR` needs the right argument to be $\mathsf{id}$, but $U_3 \neq \mathsf{id}$ -- does not fire; `DExtn` needs the left argument to be `Ext`-headed -- does not fire; `VarRef`/`VarSkip` need the left argument to be `Var`-headed -- does not fire; `DApp` needs the left argument to be `App`-headed -- does not fire; `Eps-eps` needs the left argument to be `Eps`-headed -- does not fire. So no base rule fires at the root. By the induction hypothesis, $U_1$ and $U_3$ are free of $\sigma$-redexes, so no congruence rule (`Lam`, `CompL`, `CompR`) fires inside them either. Irreducible.

- **$M = x \circ W$** (with $W$ a $\sigma$-normal form, $W \neq \mathsf{id}$, $W$ not an environment extension). As above, `Assoc`, `IdL`, `DExtn`, `DApp`, `Eps-eps` all require the left argument to have a shape other than `Var`, so none fires. `IdR` does not fire since $W \neq \mathsf{id}$. `VarRef` and `VarSkip` both require the *right* argument to be `Ext`-headed, but $W$ is explicitly not an environment extension, so neither fires. So no base rule fires at the root. By the induction hypothesis, $W$ is free of $\sigma$-redexes (and $x$, being a variable, has no subterm to reduce), so no congruence rule fires inside either. Irreducible.

### ($\Rightarrow$) Every $\sigma$-irreducible term is generated by (2.32).

By structural induction on $M$, ranging over all seven constructors of $\mathbf{Term}(\lambda_{\mathrm{Env}\varepsilon})$ (`docs/enve-syntax.md` §2.1), assuming $M$ is $\sigma$-irreducible.

- **$M = \mathsf{id}$.** Matches the `id` production directly.

- **$M = x$.** Matches the `x` production directly.

- **$M = \lambda x.\,M_1$.** If $M_1$ had a $\sigma$-redex, the `Lam` congruence rule would let $M$ reduce (at that redex, inside $M_1$), contradicting irreducibility of $M$. So $M_1$ is $\sigma$-irreducible; by the induction hypothesis, $M_1$ is a $\sigma$-normal form $U_1$. So $M = \lambda x.\,U_1$ matches the grammar.

- **$M = M_1 M_2$.** If either $M_1$ or $M_2$ had a $\sigma$-redex, `AppL` or `AppR` would apply. So both are $\sigma$-irreducible, hence (by the induction hypothesis) $\sigma$-normal forms $U_1, U_2$, and $M = U_1 U_2$ matches the grammar.

- **$M = (M_1/x)\cdot M_2$.** Symmetric to the previous case, using `ExtnL`/`ExtnR`: $M_1, M_2$ are $\sigma$-normal forms $U_1, U_2$, and $M = (U_1/x)\cdot U_2$ matches the grammar.

- **$M = \varepsilon(M_1)$.** If $M_1$ had a $\sigma$-redex, `Eop` would apply. So $M_1$ is a $\sigma$-normal form $U_1$, and $M = \varepsilon(U_1)$ matches the grammar.

- **$M = M_1 \circ M_2$.** If either $M_1$ or $M_2$ had a $\sigma$-redex, `CompL` or `CompR` would apply. So both are $\sigma$-irreducible, hence (by the induction hypothesis) $\sigma$-normal forms -- in particular, $M_1$'s head constructor is one of the eight top-level shapes already covered by this very grammar. Since $M$ itself is irreducible, none of the base rules may fire at the root of $M_1 \circ M_2$ either, which rules out several of those eight shapes for $M_1$:
    - If $M_1 = \mathsf{id}$, `IdL` would fire -- excluded.
    - If $M_1 = (U_1'/x)\cdot U_2'$ (an environment extension), `DExtn` would fire -- excluded.
    - If $M_1 = U_1' U_2'$ (an application), `DApp` would fire -- excluded.
    - If $M_1 = \varepsilon(U_1')$, `Eps-eps` would fire -- excluded.
    - If $M_1$ is itself `Comp`-headed (either of the two `Comp`-shaped grammar productions), `Assoc` would fire -- excluded.

  This leaves exactly two possible shapes for $M_1$: $M_1 = \lambda x.\,U_1$ (for some $\sigma$-normal form $U_1$), or $M_1 = x$ (a variable). In either case, since $M$ is irreducible, `IdR` cannot fire, so $M_2 \neq \mathsf{id}$.

    - If $M_1 = \lambda x.\,U_1$: setting $U_3 := M_2$ (a $\sigma$-normal form with $U_3 \neq \mathsf{id}$), $M = (\lambda x.\,U_1) \circ U_3$ matches the grammar.
    - If $M_1 = x$: since $M$ is irreducible, `VarRef` and `VarSkip` cannot fire, so $M_2$ is not an environment extension. Setting $W := M_2$ (a $\sigma$-normal form, $W \neq \mathsf{id}$, $W$ not an environment extension), $M = x \circ W$ matches the grammar.

This covers every constructor, completing the induction.

#### End of Proof.

## Remarks and Adjustments Made to the Source Material

- **Proof supplied in full.** The scanned source states thesis Theorem 6 but its proof was not included in the material provided for this task, so this document supplies a complete proof (both directions, by structural induction), derived directly from the rules of `docs/enve-reduction-sigma.md` and cross-checked case by case against the eight productions of grammar (2.32).
- **No content issues found in the grammar itself.** Working out, rule by rule, exactly which shapes of a `Comp`-headed term $M_1 \circ M_2$ avoid all eight base $\sigma$-rules confirms precisely the two `Comp` productions of (2.32) (`Lam`-headed left argument with $U_3 \neq \mathsf{id}$; `Var`-headed left argument with $W$ neither $\mathsf{id}$ nor an environment extension) and no others, and confirms that no side condition is needed for the other six productions (`id`, `x`, `Lam`, `App`, `Ext`, `Eps`) beyond their immediate subterms being $\sigma$-normal forms. No corrections to the grammar itself were necessary.
- **No constants involved.** As with every other reduction document in this repository, this lemma does not involve `Const` or any constant symbols, since $\lambda_{\mathrm{Env}\varepsilon}$ has none (`docs/enve-syntax.md`).
